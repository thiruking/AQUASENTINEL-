Water Quality Parameter Glossary

Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Air for Fish

What is this?

Dissolved oxygen is the invisible oxygen trapped in water that fish and shrimp breathe through their gills.

Why does it matter?

Just like humans need fresh air, aquatic life depends on high oxygen levels to stay active, eat food, and grow fast.

What if it's too low?

If oxygen drops below 4.0 mg/L, fish gasp at the surface, stop eating, and can suffocate within hours.

Current: 6.5 mg/L Ideal Zone: 5.0 - 9.0 mg/L

Ammonia (NH3) Waste & Toxin

What is this?

Ammonia is a toxic waste product created from fish droppings and uneaten food rotting at the pond bottom.

Why does it matter?

High ammonia damages fish gills and burns their skin, making them sick and vulnerable to disease.

What if it's too high?

Keep ammonia under 0.05 mg/L. High levels stop growth and cause sudden mass mortality.

Current: 0.02 mg/L Safe: < 0.05 mg/L

pH Level Water Sourness

What is this?

pH measures whether water is too acidic (sour) or too alkaline (soapy). Think of clean drinking tap water.

Why does it matter?

Shrimp and fish thrive in stable, slightly alkaline water (pH 7.5 to 8.5).

What if it fluctuates?

Sudden pH swings stress shrimp molting and weaken fish skin immunity.

Current: 7.8 pH Ideal Zone: 7.5 - 8.5

Turbidity Water Clarity

What is this?

Turbidity measures how cloudy or muddy the water looks due to suspended algae and mud particles.

Why does it matter?

Good cloudiness (25-45 NTU) means healthy green plankton food for shrimp. Too muddy blocks sunlight.

What if it's too high?

Excess mud clogs fish gills and reduces natural oxygen production.

Current: 35 NTU Ideal Zone: 25 - 45 NTU

Water Temperature Pond Warmth

What is this?

The warmth of the pond water, influenced by sunlight, shade, and weather seasons.

Why does it matter?

Fish are cold-blooded; temperature dictates how fast they digest food and grow.

What if it's too hot/cold?

Above 32°C water loses oxygen rapidly; below 22°C fish stop eating completely.

Current: 28.5 °C Ideal Zone: 26 - 31 °C

Salinity Salt Content

What is this?

The amount of natural salt dissolved in the coastal water.

Why does it matter?

Coastal shrimp require moderate salt water (10-25 ppt) to build strong shells.

What if heavy rains occur?

Heavy rainfall dilutes salt levels rapidly, causing shrimp shell molting stress.

Current: 15 ppt Ideal Zone: 10 - 25 ppt
